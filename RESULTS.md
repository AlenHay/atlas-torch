## Noise floor: compiler variants (decision, M0)

* Variants: gfortran -O0/-O3, ifx -O0/-O2/-O3/`-fp-model precise`, all with the stock SAVE/zero-init semantics,
  built by `scripts/build_fortran.sh`.
* **DFSYNTHE/KAPPA9 have only ifx variants.** xnfdf/dfsynthe/dfsortp/kappa9 are written in Intel/DEC dialect
  (`OPEN(TYPE=..., READONLY, SHARED)`, `RECORDTYPE/BLOCKSIZE`, `TYPE*` output, rank-mismatched EQUIVALENCE,
  tab-format lines longer than 72 columns) and `RECL` for direct access is in 4-byte words under ifx but bytes
  under gfortran: a gfortran build would differ in I/O semantics, not floating-point noise. Their spread is
  therefore measured across ifx optimisation levels and fp models only (`tools/gf_shim.py` exists for
  best-effort gfortran builds; not used for tolerances). `rmolecasc` (Hollerith assignment ifx rejects) is
  built with gfortran -O3 in the ifx variants; it only reformats line lists.
* SYNTHE-family sources use direct-access files with RECL in bytes (gfortran convention): the ifx variants
  of that family are built with `-assume byterecl` (I/O units only; first attempt crashed in rh2ofast).
* ATLAS9 stock = gfortran -O0, so the gf-O0 variant is identical to stock by construction; the spread comes
  from gf-O3 and the ifx builds.
* Stages are scored stage-isolated: variant ATLAS9 runs use the stock ODFs, variant SYNTHE runs the stock solar
  model, KAPPA9-alone stages (`kappa9/<mix>`) the stock ODF.
* Tolerance rule (frozen once in `results/tolerances.json` by `tools/score/noise_floor.py --write`, before any
  torch result exists): `tol = max(3 x max-over-variants error vs stock, 1 quantum of the output format)`.
  The quantum (1 ulp for binary float64, last printed digit for text tables, 1 step for integer ODF/kapk
  tables) only matters when every variant agrees exactly. This session created this rule.

### DFSYNTHE and flush-to-zero (decision, M0, made before any full torch ODF existed)

* Stock DFSYNTHE/KAPPA9 is ifx with the default `-fp-model fast`, which runs with flush-to-zero/denormals-are-zero
  (FTZ/DAZ) and Intel's SVML. ifx `-O0` and `-fp-model precise` builds do not flush.
* Measured on the solar mix: ifx-O3 vs stock differs by at most 1 ODF step (1-2 entries out of 5.6 M), but
  ifx-O0 and ifx-precise differ by up to **3590 steps (3.6 dex)** in 0.35 % of entries, concentrated at cool
  temperatures (index 0-17, < 4400 K), low pressures and some frequency intervals — whole ODF rows shifted by
  ~3.3 dex, i.e. molecular opacity switched on/off, not rounding; kappa.ros differs by 0.005 dex.
* FTZ is real in xnfdf (vs stock, 13 586 `xnfpdf.dat` values are 0 in stock but nonzero at -O0; an
  `ifx -O0 -ftz` build removes that category), so the DFSYNTHE/KAPPA9 noise variants are run in stock's FTZ
  environment: `ifx-O3` (FTZ by default), `ifx-precise-ftz`, `ifx-O0-ftz`. **But FTZ is not the cause of the
  ODF jumps** (correction of an earlier note): `ifx-precise-ftz` still differs from stock by up to 3300-3590
  steps in ~0.3 % of entries, while its xnfdf output differs from stock only at rounding level. The jumps
  originate inside dfsynthe under `-fp-model fast` (root cause under investigation). Even at T >= 4571 K the
  precise builds reach 119 (solar) / 358 (alpha) steps somewhere: cool-and-warm ODFs are genuinely not
  reproducible across the Fortran's own compiler settings at the level of single entries.
* Consequence for gating (decided before any full-table torch ODF exists): the literal max-abs gate stays
  (3 x spread, i.e. ~±10 dex — recorded, but it gates almost nothing), and distributional metrics under the
  same 3 x rule carry the weight: `share_gt1` (fraction of entries off by > 1 step; Fortran spread ~0.3 %),
  `p999` (99.9th percentile |diff|; spread 24 steps), max-abs over T >= 4571 K, and kappa.ros max-abs.
  This session created both these metrics and the gate.
* Seen before freezing: the DFSYNTHE port agent reported one temperature (5012 K): port vs stock max 18 steps,
  vs ifx-precise 1 step (the port reproduces the non-FTZ precise build). No full-table torch ODF exists yet.

### Root cause of DFSYNTHE's cool-temperature ODF jumps (found 2026-11-04)

* One statement in HPROF4 (label 52, the Lyman-alpha quasi-H2+ term
  `HPROF4=HPROF4+CUTOFF4000/PRQSP4000*PRQS/FO(J)*DBETA*1.77245*DOP`, `PRQSP4000=SOFBET(BETA4000,...)*.5/FO(J)*DBETA`).
  With `-fp-model fast` ifx inlines PRQSP4000 and evaluates the term as one numerator/denominator pair; at low
  electron density both products fall below FLT_MIN, FTZ flushes them and the term becomes 0/0 = NaN (solar
  2512 K: layers 1-7). The NaN then propagates through stock's compare/MAX instructions (`KAPPA.LT.CONTINUUM`
  false, `maxss`/`maxps` select the non-NaN operand) so that **stock drops all line opacity in the sampled bins
  blue of Lyman-alpha in those layers**. Precise/-O0 builds evaluate left to right and do not.
* Evidence: H lines alone / Lyman lines alone / Ly-alpha alone each reproduce the full 6438-step difference; a
  stock-flags build instrumented to print HPROF4 shows NaN there (and the NaN disappears when intermediates are
  printed, i.e. the evaluation order changes); the machine code confirms the order.
* The reference (stock) therefore contains a numerical artefact that the port reproduces deliberately
  (`Flavor('stock')` in src/atlas_torch/dfsynthe, together with the other fast-math forms: VTTAB, TABVOIGT/SVML,
  `rcpps` divisions with the reference CPU's measured table). With them the port is within 1 step of stock in all
  but 5e-5 of the 5012 K entries (18 steps / 4.7e-4 without). Physically the precise builds are the more
  defensible ODFs; BasicATLAS users get the stock behaviour.
* Speed: the cool temperatures need ~2.5e11 profile points; the line accumulation runs in a small C kernel
  (literal transcription of the Fortran loop, compiled in the container, loaded via ctypes, bit-identical to the
  torch path, which stays as the portable fallback). Revisit in M7 (a pure-torch/GPU path is the brief's target).

### Hot-temperature DFSYNTHE differences (found and fixed 2026-11-04)

* 12589 K / 22387 K (up to 15 / 41 steps): the Lyman-alpha quasi-H2+ far-wing term in HPROF4
  (`CUTOFF=(10.**(CUTOFF-14.))/2.99792458E10*XNFPH(J,2); HPROF4=HPROF4+CUTOFF*1.77245*DOP`). The port followed the
  source order and formed `10.**(CUTOFF-14.)/C`, a subnormal value that FTZ flushes 13100-15000 cm^-1 red of the
  centre (dense hot layers lost up to 5 % of their XLINOP opacity there). The stock binary evaluates
  `((XNFPH(J,2)*K)*DOP)*10.**(CUTOFF-14.)` with `K=(1/C)*1.77245` folded and never forms the subnormal.
* 112202 K (119 steps): not FTZ. Stock computes the merging edge as `XNE**(-2./15.)*1600.-1.5` with Intel SVML `powf`
  and `(1/NMERGE**2)*109737.312` (rcpps + Newton in the vector loop); one rounding step decides whether the far red
  wings of four He II Lyman lines just below WCON start.
* Both reproduced in the stock flavour (torch path and C kernel, bit-identical to each other). Since then both ODF
  mixes are within 1 step of stock at all 57 temperatures (share > 1 step: 0, as ifx-O3), kappa.ros identical.
  Remaining 1-step flips (6e-5 of entries, vs 0-2e-6 for ifx-O3) come from unemulated rounding-level fast-math forms.
  The M7 GPU section's DFSYNTHE 12589 K row predates this fix.

### Findings so far

* Correction (line readers, commit 44c6701): the claim "byte-identical except TABLOG fields" was wrong — the
  rmolecasc/rgfalllines ports did REAL*4 arithmetic in float64 (up to 79 ulp in ~600k records; the stage test hid
  it behind a 5e-4 helper tolerance). Fixed: all line programs now byte-identical to stock on the four reference
  windows (incl. TABLOG via libmvec powf, which only rh2ofast uses), and the test requires exact equality.
* Host vs container: the host glibc 2.43 `log10f` differs from the container's 2.39; all references and scores
  are produced inside the container.

* KAPPA9 alone: all four ifx variants reproduce the stock `kapk*.dat` and `kappa.ros` byte-for-byte (solar and
  alpha), so the KAPPA9 gate is one print quantum (1 milli-dex step / 0.001 dex).
* SYNTHE `spectrv`: a gfortran -O0 debug build reproduces the -O3 stock `spectrum.bin` bit-for-bit (10 nm window).
* Our solar ODF vs the one shipped in BasicATLAS `data/solar_ODF`: 0.6 % of ODF steps differ (up to 3.6 dex in
  rare bins), `kappa.ros` within 0.001 dex — the shipped tables come from other line-list versions;
  information only, the gate is our pinned build.

Study only: no default changed, no file under `src/` edited. Scripts: `tools/bench/gpu_*.py` (docstrings say how
to re-run each; `tools/bench/gpu_run.sh cpu|gpu <script>` picks the interpreter inside the container), raw results

Measured 2026-11-04 on the shared machine (load average 3-9, other agents changing `src/` at the same time; the code
was commit f6a611f plus their uncommitted work), so **the timings are indicative only**. Re-run them on a quiet
machine; the accuracy results do not depend on load.

**Modes.** *exact* = the default path (glibc/libmvec emulation, C kernels, FTZ; CPU float64).
*native* = CPU float64 with native torch math, using the package's own switches (`atlas9.physics.libm.EXACT`,
`synthe.opacity._core.EXACT`, `libm32.native() -> None` (the documented non-x86 fallback), SPECTRV `libm="torch"`,
`atlas9.fmath`/`josh` glibc helpers patched to torch, DFSYNTHE without its C kernels). *gpu64* = the same on the GPU
(AMD RDNA3 8 GB, torch 2.9.1+rocm6.4, `HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION=11.0.0`). *cpu32/gpu32* = every float64 (REAL*8)
torch op computed in float32 (`gpu_common.F32Emu`: a TorchFunctionMode that runs sums, matmuls and
transcendentals natively in float32 and rounds every other float64 result to float32; inputs are read as float32;
the REAL*4 code paths are unchanged). *cpu32p/gpu32p* (diagnostic) = float32 precision (24-bit significand) with
float64 exponent range, which separates rounding failures from underflow/overflow. Per program, each torch program
runs on the stock inputs of that program and the rest of the chain is the stock Fortran, so the stage metric
measures that program alone. All stages are scored with `atlas_torch.scoring` against the frozen
`results/tolerances.json`. Single CPU thread throughout.

Plain answers:
* **CPU float64 native math:** all four SYNTHE windows (each program and the whole chain), KAPPA9, and DFSYNTHE stay
  within the gates, mostly byte-identical: SYNTHE writes REAL*4 spectra, so ulp-level differences vanish.
  ATLAS9 stays within the gates on 5 of 6 models (worst 0.78 of a gate). It **fails sun**: FLXDRV differs by 0.01,
  FLXERR by 1e-5 and P by 1.1e-6 rel, one printed digit each, but sun's FLXDRV/FLXERR/HEIGHT gates are zero
  because every Fortran variant printed the same digits there.
* **GPU float64:** same verdict as CPU native. SYNTHE: byte-identical or ≤0.07 of a gate (w3 synthe); the GPU
  line-opacity sums are float32 atomics, so the summation order differs. KAPPA9: byte-identical. DFSYNTHE
  12589 K: ≤1 step. ATLAS9: t4500g25 within the gates (0.18), sun fails by one printed digit (FLXDRV 0.01,
  FLXERR 1e-5, HEIGHT 0.001 km), the same as CPU native.
* **float32 for the REAL*8 parts (CPU or GPU, same result): not viable.** Only the SYNTHE line-opacity program,
  which is REAL*4 in the Fortran anyway, survives in w0 and w2 (0.095, 0.82 of a gate). It fails w1 (4.8×) and w3
  (8.3×): its REAL*8 wavelength grid matters at 380-420 nm and 1.5-1.6 µm. Failures by stage:
  * xnfpelsyn: 42-550× the gate. Trace populations fall below FLT_MIN (3929 of 30215 XNFPEL entries flushed to 0),
    and contscat is off by up to 4.5 dex.
  * spectrv: 1.5-44×.
  * KAPPA9: the Rosseland table overflows its print field (`*****`).
  * ATLAS9 sun: T 4.5× the gate, P/ρ/XNE ~1e-3 vs 1e-5, flux error 0.85 %.
  * DFSYNTHE 12589 K: up to 40 dex, with 14 % of entries off by more than 1 step.
  * XNFDF populations in float32 make DFSYNTHE's C kernel segfault, and the GPU float32 run stops with
    `bincount minlength < 0`.

  The precision-only diagnostic still fails almost everywhere (xnfpelsyn 6-31×, spectrv 1.5-44×, ATLAS9 sun,
  KAPPA9 at its 1-quantum gate). So it is the 24-bit significand, not just the exponent range: rescaling or log
  storage alone would not rescue float32.

### 5. Side finding for the DFSYNTHE owner (not a study change)

At 12589 K (IT=33) the default CPU path with FTZ differs from stock by up to 14-15 steps (5e-4 of the entries

### Recommendations

1. Keep float64. float32 for REAL*8 quantities fails every stage that has them, and precision is the cause, not
   only range.
2. Expose one GPU option, SYNTHE `synthe_prog(device='cuda')`:
   * 2.5-7× faster than the current CPU exact path on the 10-40 nm windows;
   * within the gates on all four windows (≤0.07 of a gate);
   * not byte-identical (float32 atomics), so not a default;
   * worth more for the wide BasicATLAS batches (up to 2 M points).

   Measure it on a 100-3000 nm example before deciding. DFSYNTHE `lines_device` is slower than the C kernel and
   brings nothing.
3. Making xnfpelsyn/KAPPA9/ATLAS9 device-clean means:
   * the `save_get` default-device fix;
   * a device argument threaded through READIN/POPS and the `atlas9.physics` tables (≈50-60 sites listed).

   It brings no speed on its own (launch-bound). It matters for the batched-model / differentiable work: a
   leading model axis is what would make the GPU useful there.
4. Use torch.compile only for SPECTRV's JOSH on the CPU (2.2×, bit-identical) if SPECTRV ever becomes a
   bottleneck. Elsewhere it gives no gain or breaks identity.

### API

```python

ch.structure_tangents()["teff"]["T"]       # dT(layer)/dTeff of the converged ATLAS9 model

```

number fractions renormalised), `mh` (ABUNDANCE SCALE), `vturb` [km/s], per-layer `T`, `P`, `XNE`, `RHOX`.
Example: `examples/gradients_demo.py`; validation driver: `python -m atlas_torch.diff.validate`.

### Design (what is differentiated, and how)

* **Differentiable mode = the exact path's formulas in float64 with native torch math.** The port's libm
  emulation is switched to torch kernels through its existing `EXACT` flags inside `diff.native.native_math()`
  (context manager, restores everything on exit); PFIRON's REAL*4 interpolation runs in float64 (table cells
  chosen as in REAL*4). XNFPELSYN's physics (PFSAHA, KAPP and all continuum opacity kernels) is the port's own
  torch code run as is; POPS is re-implemented in `diff.xnf` only so that XABUND stays a tensor. SYNTHE's line
  loop (`diff.lines`), XLINOP's hydrogen lines with HPROF4 (`diff.hydrogen`) and SPECTRV/JOSH (`diff.rt`) are
  float64 transcriptions.
* **Tabulated functions** (FASTEX exp table, H0/H1/H2TAB, VOIGT): the value is the exact path's table entry
  (same REAL*4 index arithmetic), the derivative that of the function the table samples ("straight-through",
  `profile="exact"`, default). `profile="smooth"` evaluates the smooth functions themselves.
* **Discrete decisions are taken once at the base point and held fixed**: SYNTHE's two cutoff tests per
  (line, layer) and the profile extents (core exit, far-wing MAXSTEP), the Balmer-wing walks, JOSH's
  thick/interval choices. The derivative is that of the smooth piece containing the base point (it ignores
  contributions switching on/off at the 1e-4*continuum cutoff). `sd(params, redecide=True)` re-takes them.
* **JOSH**: the scattering problem on the 51-point grid is solved exactly (float64 LU) instead of REAL*4
  Gauss-Seidel sweeps stopped at |dS/S| < 1e-5.
* **NMOLEC: implicit differentiation at convergence** (`diff.nmolec`). The Fortran's Newton iteration is
  replayed exactly (bit-identical iterate, checked); then, per layer, X = X* + (D - D.detach()) with
  D = -J*^-1 EQ(X*; theta), J* the analytic Jacobian at X*: the primal is the exact iterate, the tangent is
  -J^-1 dEQ/dtheta (IFT), never through the iterations. Two Fortran details couple layers: the ion
  equilibrium constants use the *start* XNE(J) = XNE*(J-1)*P(J)/P(J-1) (Debye lowering), so the tangent solve is a
  forward substitution over layers; CPF uses the deck XNE. `polish=True` converges the root to machine
  precision first (smooth forward function, used for the FD checks).
* **Forward-mode AD** (`torch.autograd.forward_ad`, one JVP per parameter direction): ~1e4 outputs, few
  parameters; and it runs through the port's torch opacity kernels unchanged. Reverse mode was tried first and
  gives NaN gradients: `torch.where` over masked non-finite branches (e.g. H2COLLOP's 10**x beyond its
  table) makes 0*inf products in backward; forward mode never forms them.

### ATLAS9: implicit differentiation of the converged model (`diff.atlas9_ift`)

Fixed-point map: x = the restart deck (RHOX, T, P, XNE, ABROSS, ACCRAD per layer, PRADK0; 433 numbers, in full
float64 precision: the punched deck has 4-9 digits, so x replaces READ PUNCH's columns in memory), G = one
iteration of the port's ATLAS9 from x with theta = (Teff, log g, [M/H] = log10 ABUNDANCE SCALE); ODF, VTURB
and the abundance pattern fixed ([M/H] is therefore the partial derivative at fixed line opacity). One
iteration: 5-14 s depending on load (pre-M7 code). Probe (all layers of one quantity x(1+1e-3)): G depends on
input RHOX (2.8e-3) and T (6.1e-3) only; P, XNE, ABROSS have exactly zero influence (recomputed), ACCRAD 1e-7,
PRADK0 3e-9. dG/dtheta by central differences (Teff +/-2 K, log g and [M/H] +/-0.002).

Solvers for dx*/dtheta = (I - dG/dx)^-1 dG/dtheta, tried in this order:

1. **Dense FD Jacobian** of one iteration (the brief's suggestion; 144 RHOX/T columns, central differences,
   ~300 iterations, 12-20 min with 2 workers), steps 1e-5 and 2e-4 relative: **unusable**. The two Jacobians
   give different, unphysical tangents (dT/dTeff = -1.1 resp. -0.5 at the top layer; physical: +0.6), and a
   chord-Newton iteration with either Jacobian diverges. Diagnosis: (i) G is smooth to 1e-9 along single
   columns at the 1e-5 scale, but TCORR maps the corrected model back onto the standard tau grid with MAP1, and
   at the fixed point the model's own tau points coincide with that grid, so G is only piecewise smooth there
   and per-column derivatives do not combine into the response to a collective perturbation (directional FD
   along the IFT direction differs from J.v by 10-12% at 0.1-1 K amplitude; dG/dTeff from +/-0.1 K steps is
   0.16 at the top layer, from +/-1 K and +/-5 K 0.028); (ii) I - dG/dx is ill-conditioned (largest
   eigenvalues of dG/dx 0.9973-0.998, cond ~1e6) with the slow modes localised at the convective boundary
   (layers 56-64), where even the restoring slope scatters by +/-50% with the step (fixed point undetermined
   along that mode at ~7e-4 relative, ~5 K).
2. **Jacobian-free GMRES** (operator v -> v - J v with directional central differences, ~1 K amplitude; 8-13
   products, 10 min for three parameters): accurate for the dominant P/RHOX responses to log g and [M/H]
   (1-2%), not for the small T responses (the operator is only piecewise linear; true residual 1-9%).
3. **Picard linear response** (default): t <- J t + dG/dtheta, J t by directional central differences with a
   fixed amplitude, 40 steps = the linearised ATLAS9 iteration itself (2 x 40 iterations per parameter,
   15-19 min each at load ~6-10). Robust to the kinks; converges at ATLAS9's rate (40 steps leave the slow
   convective mode partly unconverged, as for ATLAS9 itself).

### Limitations

* Decisions are frozen: the derivative is that of the piece of the (piecewise smooth) SYNTHE function at the
  base point; cutoff switchings (1e-4 of the continuum) and MAP1/PARCOE kinks are not represented.
* fort.19 line types other than hydrogen (He I, autoionising, merged continua, TYPE 0/3 Voigt lines) and fort.12
  lines with ALPHA != 0 raise NotImplementedError (none in the reference windows' blue part checked here;
  the Ca H&K/H-alpha windows were not run). SURFACE FLUX only (IFSURF=1); velocity fields with fort.19 lines
  unsupported; air wavelengths with H series merging unsupported.
* XNE of the SYNTHE deck has zero influence (by construction of the Fortran), so its derivative is 0.
* The ATLAS9 part uses finite differences of the exact-path iteration (not autograd); its accuracy is limited
  by ATLAS9's own piecewise structure and slow convective-boundary convergence (table above). [M/H] is at
  fixed ODF. Cost is comparable to re-converging perturbed models (the IFT's advantage over FD is that one
  linear response serves every downstream quantity, e.g. the spectrum, without re-running SYNTHE per model).
* Reverse mode (gradients of a scalar loss in one pass) is not supported through KAPP (NaN from masked
  branches); use forward mode (few parameters) or structure_jacobian.
